Search Intent & Keyword Mapping: Eliminating Keyword Cannibalization

Direct Summary (Centerpiece Answer)

Keyword mapping assigns every distinct search intent to exactly one primary URL on your website, preventing keyword cannibalization where multiple internal pages split authority and confuse Google about which page should rank.

Supports Core Service: On-Page SEO Services Nepal · Published by Dharan SEO Consultancy, Dharan, Nepal · Updated 2026-11-23

The Four Core Search Intent Categories

  • Transactional / Commercial: Ready to hire, request a quote, or buy (maps to Core Money Pages like /seo-services-dharan/).
  • Local Commercial: Seeking a nearby verified provider (maps to /local-seo-services-dharan/ and GBP).
  • Commercial Investigation: Comparing options, costs, or vetting criteria (maps to comparison and buyer guides).
  • Informational: Learning how a concept or process works (maps to Outer Section Knowledge Hub articles).

How to Diagnose and Fix Keyword Cannibalization

Inspect Google Search Console performance reports filtered by a specific query and view the Pages tab. If two or more URLs trade impressions back and forth while stalling on page two, decide which URL owns the intent: either merge and 301 redirect the weaker duplicate into the primary page, or refocus one page cleanly onto an informational sub-intent and link it up to the commercial page. See our On-Page SEO Services Nepal for full site mapping.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can one page rank for multiple related secondary keywords?

Yes. A single well-structured page naturally ranks for dozens of closely related variations sharing the same search intent; separate pages are only needed when user intent genuinely diverges.

Should blog posts target the exact same primary keyword as my service page?

No. Blog posts should target informational or comparative sub-questions and pass contextual relevance upward to your service page via internal links.
